When we watch dance in a theater, we share the same space and time as the dancer and feel the presence of their body. But when we watch dance through media on a screen, we perceive the body in the frame as one different from our own. This work tests whether, even in a virtual space where time and space are not shared, we can sense a dancer's physicality through everyday sensations.

Everyday movement was divided into three emotions (anger, sadness, happiness) and four basic states (lying, standing, sitting, moving). Eleven movements within them were archived as dance videos and arranged on the web.

Viewers enter the path their own body traced that day as a combination of movements and emotions, and the dance piece begins from that combination. Watching familiar, repeated gestures from the distance of the audience seat, viewers sense anew the movements they usually pass over, and go on to experience their own body expanding.

The work starts from the idea that physicality arises not only from sharing time and space but also through the transfer of empathy. It asks whether the experience of everyday memories surfacing one after another in a virtual space can move beyond the limits of physicality in media and become a new way of watching.

[Theoretical Background]

Physicality is the materiality that proves, through the dancer's movement, that the dancer's body exists in that time and space. Physicality can be felt 1) through the sharing of time and space, and 2) through the transfer of empathy. Physicality through shared time and space arises from sharing the same space, the theater, at the same time; it is the process by which the audience reads the dancer's movements on stage and objectively perceives the presence and materiality of the body. Physicality through the transfer of empathy, by contrast, refers to receiving and appreciating physicality transferred from the dancer, based on shared sensations, through the narrative or message of the piece or through other media.

Media has repeatability in that it can be replayed, whereas performance has the particularity of being one-time. In media where the boundary between dance and media has dissolved, such as performance film, time is not shared and spatial immersion is excluded, so unlike conventional dance performance, the sharing of physicality is limited. We perceive the physicality of media within a frame in virtual space as different from that of a real body. Then through what materiality, and what transferred sensations, can we overcome these limits of physicality?
