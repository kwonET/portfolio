This work questions the social and scientific consensus on where life begins, and the belief that humans stand at the top of the food chain. Humans place themselves at the peak of the food chain, yet after death they are eaten by flies and maggots. Within this cycle, the work deals with how ambiguous the order and perspective of birth and death, predator and prey, really are.

Images of organisms such as fetuses, frog eggs, and maggots were collected and refined, transformed into particles, and projection-mapped onto an immersive space. The dancer moves along the flow of the food chain, contrasting with the form of each living being.

After the performance, the audience sends back to the work, via the web, the shape and order of the food chain they experienced. Their participation shapes the food chain and the dancer's gestures in the next performance. Rather than remaining in a performance relationship where subject and object are clearly divided, the audience becomes a subject that rewrites the order of the performance. Three kinds of changing movement run through the performance: the dancer's accelerating movement, particles shifting in real time, and the audience's attempt to overturn the order of the food chain between species and the basis of life's origin. The tension over who holds the power to accept that attempt exists both inside and outside the work.

Drawing on Erika Fischer-Lichte's notion of the role reversal between audience and performer, the work seeks to translate the moment the hierarchy of the food chain is overturned into relationships on stage.
