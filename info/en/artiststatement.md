Bomi Kwon focuses on what is easily overlooked in a fast-moving, unsettled society: minority perspectives, disappearing cultures, and the subtle, invisible movements of nature. Grounded in dance theory such as Fischer-Lichte's aesthetics of the performative, Kwon experiments with ideas of dance in media, interaction between audience and dancer, and technology and dance.
Kwon's creative methodology unfolds through the following stages.

Observation / Concept : Every work begins with observation. By collecting and arranging the theoretical grounds, images, and data of a chosen concept, the artist gives concrete shape to a conceptual medium. This concept then expands into a work through the processes below.

Translation through Technology : This is the process of translating the conceptual medium into technology and media, where the concept is visualized on the basis of data. Working mainly with code and TouchDesigner, Kwon creates image collages, particles, and abstract code art. Using a range of technologies and media lets the audience see the conceptual medium more intuitively and from a perspective beyond its original dimension. Kwon aims to create new forms of art through ever-advancing technology and increasingly hybrid media.

Embodiment / Interaction : The body, the core mode of expression, serves as a performative medium. Going beyond visual expression through technology, the work invites the audience to intervene and creates sensations through the body. In this way, a concept that exists in another dimension is turned into an embodied experience. Through interaction with the work, the artist also seeks to reduce the psychological distance between the audience and the dance within the work, and art itself, and, drawing on dance theory, tests whether the audience's own actions can also become part of the work.
