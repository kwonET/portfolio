By deconstructing and reassembling the letters of shop signs (ganpan), this work explores how a single visual sign becomes a different object as it crosses from physical space to digital to print media.

Individual letters from city signs were photographed and labeled to build a digital typographic database. A sentence entered by the user is recomposed from the letters in this database, creating a sign collage that never existed before.

Users watch their sentence being rebuilt from fragments of unfamiliar signs. The generated collages are collected on the web, then printed by risograph to take the physical form of a zine. Signs from physical space are taken apart and recombined, digitized, and reborn as printed matter. Through this, the sign becomes the subject that expresses the moment when contemporary digital media interacts with the analog.

Between digital art and print media, this work proposes a way of seeing Korean spaces from multiple perspectives.
